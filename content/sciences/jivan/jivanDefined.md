- Jivan is a software that makes notes you write into queryable data. It mainly deals with cells. You just define these cells and they will interact with one another, and, without you realizing it, life will slowly emerge.
## Function
- These cells are similar to spreadsheet cells in that they contain a piece of information and take up a certain amount of space.
    - Any piece of information can be a cell. Whether it be a single sentence, a paragraph, or even 100 paragraphs.
- Jivan allows you to write the content of these cells and define the space they take up. Doing these, you can declare relationships between these cells.
    - A cell can have a hundred parents, a thousand children, and millions of spouses.
    - This is because cells are just manifestations of information. They can spatially and textually exist in many places at once, yet when the information is changed in one place it will change in all — all of them are just manifestations of the same piece of knowledge.
## The Model
- Fundamentally, the spatial rules of a cell are as follows: above is parents, below is children, horizontal is spouse. Every keystroke and interaction just updates the underlying model and the relationships are derived from that. The view is always downstream of the model.
- Splitting is what declares a relationship. Placing a cell without splitting is just arrangement — it carries no relational meaning. The act of splitting is the declaration.
    - Split vertically downward → child relationship
    - Split vertically upward → parent relationship
    - Split horizontally → spouse relationship
    - Place without splitting → meaningless arrangement, just layout organization

## Shoma
- The main view of Jivan is made up of a bunch of Shoma. Each Shoma is relative to a singular cell. It will look like a single box with text in it — similar to a traditional text editor but fundamentally different.
- Each Shoma has two ways of interacting with it:
    1. Writing content — you change the text of the cell. When you write headings, body text, and indented bullet points, those are parent and child relationships being declared on the spot. A heading is a parent, its body text a child, a sub-bullet a child of the bullet above it. You can also declare spousal relationships inline by typing the name of another cell — it will autofill and link. Both children and spouses are clickable and navigable directly from the text.
        - Note: text relationships can reach anywhere across the entire project — unlike spatial relationships which are always relative to the current cell.
    2. Defining space — you declare relationships spatially by splitting the cell following the model above. Every further split adds another relationship to the cell you split from. This splitting replicates the look of mitosis and functions similarly to tiling window managers — over time it begins to look like a living mosaic. It also supports folding to collapse and obscure parts of the layout when needed.
    ![cell](assets/cell.jpg)
- To see a child cell's own Shoma — its content and its related cells — you open it as a new Shoma in your viewport.

## Viewport
- Your viewport is a bunch of Shoma arranged freeformly. These Shoma have no bearing on the underlying data — you just pull them into your view and move them around as you please, like tabs of a workspace.
- This also means you can have multiple cells open simultaneously, referencing one while writing in another, without any of that arrangement affecting the cells or their relationships.

## Querying
- You query using Shoma itself. You create an empty Shoma and build the query spatially using the same splitting interactions you already know — split for children, split for spouses, navigate depth for grandchildren. Then press Ctrl+Enter and it returns every cell that fits that relationship structure.
    - There is no separate query language to learn. The interface for building structure is the interface for querying it.
- You only ever declare two relationship types — parent/child and spouse. Sibling, grandparent, aunt/uncle, nephew — all emerge automatically from what you declared and are all queryable.
- For example: build a Shoma with Tasks as the root, split to a child cell marked "incomplete," press Ctrl+Enter — every unfinished task across your entire project surfaces instantly. Add another split spousing "Birthday" to that child and now you are querying for incomplete birthday tasks specifically.

## Relationships at a Glance
- In Text:
    - Type a cell name inline → spousal relationship
    - Headings, body text, and indentation → parent/child relationships
- Spatially:
    - Split vertically downward → child relationship
    - Split vertically upward → parent relationship
    - Split horizontally → spouse relationship
    - Place without splitting → layout only, no relationship declared

## Application Examples
- These are all examples of what is possible with the principles of Jivan established above. 
### D&D Campaign Library
- You have a campaign chapter "Abandoned Railroad" as a cell. You have another cell named Facts. Within Facts you have child cells — "Gandalf," "owns," "blue staff" — all three spoused to each other and also set as children of "Abandoned Railroad." In another chapter you do the same — "Gandalf," "owns," "mystic sword" — spoused to each other, children of Facts again. Now you have a bunch of facts prescribed to different stories, linked together through spousehood. You can query for children of Facts that are spouses of both "Gandalf" and "owns" — returning every item Gandalf has ever been declared to own across every chapter. 
- Take it further and ask for children of Facts AND "Abandoned Railroad" spoused to Gandalf and you get every known fact about him in that arc — everything he owned, slayed, befriended. A continuously running fact-checking engine underneath your entire campaign.
### Lawyer's Case Outline
- You have a case cell with Facts, Arguments, Evidence, Witnesses as children. Each individual fact is its own cell, each piece of evidence its own cell. You spouse facts to the evidence supporting them and arguments to the facts they depend on. You place suspects and witnesses as children of location cells — building a spatial arrangement of who was where and when. Query for facts with no spouse in Evidence and every unsupported claim surfaces. 
- Query for arguments spoused to the same single fact and find everything that collapses together if that one fact gets challenged. It becomes that red string board detectives always have except now its queryable.
### World Notes
- You have parent cells for Creatures, Environments, Factions, Power System. Inside Creatures you have a Dragons cell with child cells defining temperament, egg-laying, how they affect the power system. Inside Dragons you have cells for each dragon type. One type lays a completely different kind of egg — so you remove the original egg cell from sibling-ship with this type and give it its own egg cell as both a child AND a spouse. Query for children of Creatures that have an egg child and get every egg-laying creature in the world. Query for children of Factions spoused to a specific region and get every faction present there. Dragon never got tagged type: creature — it lives inside Creatures and that position is its property. Placement is declaration. 
- Then you write long prose cells with inline links to Dragon, Ashfields, The Accord. The concepts stay defined independelty as raw ideas and this cell gives them context in teh form of a story. 
### Musician's Inspiration Log
- Every song you consume gets a cell. Inside it you have Motifs as children — specific describable ideas like "descending bassline under unresolved chord" or "rhythmic displacement on the snare" — and Emotion children like "anxious anticipation" or "hollow nostalgia." When you sit down to work on your own music you write your production docs the same way, same motif language, same emotion language. When you type "descending bassline under unresolved chord" it autofills and links to that motif cell — now that cell's grandparents are every song in your library where you logged that exact motif.
- Query for all songs spoused to "hollow nostalgia" and a 1972 Caetano Veloso record, a UK garage B-side from 2001, a Brazilian funk track, and something from your own library from three years ago all come back together. You now have a living reference pool for exactly that feeling. Query for a specific instrument — "bowed cello" — and get every song where you logged it alongside whatever emotions and motifs you paired it with. Over time the library becomes a direct line from feeling to sound to reference.
### Zoologist's Field Notes
- Every animal gets its own cell — not a category, a specific individual. "Mara" is a cell. Inside Mara you have a cell for each year of observation, and inside each year you have individual entry cells — "refused food three days, resumed after Beta left group," "showed protective behavior toward juvenile for first time," "injury to left foreleg, compensated gait observed for six weeks." Each entry is its own cell with a date in the label, building a continuous biography over years of fieldwork.
- Entries get spoused to conditions, other animals present, health states. Query for all entries in Mara's 2019 cell spoused to "Beta present" and get every logged moment those two intersected that year. Query across all years for entries spoused to "injury" and trace exactly how Mara's behavior shifted around physical vulnerability across her entire life in the study. The field notes were always there. Now they are a navigable life story.
### Screenwriter's Show Diagram
- You have a series cell with Characters, Plot Events, Settings as children. "Elena" is a character cell. "The Bridge Confrontation" is a plot event cell. "The Northern Quarter" is a setting cell. Elena gets spoused to The Bridge Confrontation. The Northern Quarter gets spoused to The Bridge Confrontation. Elena gets spoused to The Northern Quarter. Marcus gets spoused to The Bridge Confrontation too. You can write long detailed notes about each character as child cells maybe referencing locations in text, thus spousing those two as well. 
- Every time something happens in The Northern Quarter that event gets spoused to that setting. Every time Elena and Marcus share a scene those event cells get spoused to both. Query for all plot events spoused to both Elena and Marcus and get every scene they have ever shared. Query for all plot events spoused to The Northern Quarter and get the complete history of that location. Query for settings spoused to Marcus but not Elena and find every place he exists in the world she has never entered. The characters, the world, the story — all built from declared intersections, all queryable in any direction.
### Epidemiologist Case Analysis
- You are tracking an outbreak. Location cells contain patient cells as children. Inside each patient live Symptoms, Exposure History, Timeline as children. Symptom cells get spoused across every patient who shares them. Query for the most severe symptom cells and ask what grandparent they share — patient cells come back. Ask what great-grandparent those patients share and location cells surface. That is your epicenter. Query for all children of that location and you have everyone exposed.
- Now go further. Take those patient cells and query for their symptom children spoused to an incubation period cell of under 48 hours. Cross that with a query for patients who are children of a second location cell across town. Two clusters, fast incubation, different locations — you now have evidence of a carrier moving between sites before becoming symptomatic. That connection was sitting in the structure the whole time.
### Chef's Living Cookbook
- Every dish is a cell. Inside it you have an Ingredients cell and a Procedures cell as children. Inside Ingredients you have individual ingredient cells — "lemon" is a cell. Inside Procedures you have individual procedure cells — "caramelized" is a cell, child of Procedures AND spoused to "lemon," declaring that in this dish lemon was caramelized. Another dish has "lemon" spoused to "reduced." Another has it spoused to "raw finish." Every dish built this way, every ingredient married to its procedure.
- Query for all dishes where "lemon" is spoused to "caramelized" and every dish across your body of work where you caramelized lemon comes back. Query for what other ingredients those dishes share and "fennel" appears in four of them, "brown butter" in six. You had a caramelized lemon, fennel, and brown butter instinct you never consciously named. Now query for dishes where "fennel" is spoused to "raw" instead and find the counterpoint — the same ingredient treated completely differently, a different flavor logic running parallel. Cross query for dishes that share both logics in the same recipe and find the ones where you were already bridging them without realizing it. A master chef's intuition built over decades now has a structure you can navigate, query, and deliberately build from.
### Political Science Second Brain
- Every thinker is a cell — Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Marx, Rawls, Foucault. Inside each you have Concepts, Works, Critiques as children. Every real law, historical event, institution is also its own cell — the First Amendment, the French Revolution, the New Deal, mass incarceration, the Patriot Act. Everything gets spoused to everything it touches across thinkers, centuries, and contexts.
- Hobbes's "Leviathan" concept gets spoused to the First Amendment, to Rawls's "veil of ignorance," to the Patriot Act. The French Revolution gets spoused to Rousseau, to Marx, to Burke's critique, to a modern protest movement. Over semesters and years of study every concept, thinker, event and law becomes a node in a dense web. Query for all cells spoused to both Hobbes and a modern surveillance law and trace a 17th century idea embedded in today's legislation. Query for cells spoused to both Foucault and Marx and find exactly where a poststructuralist and a communist are pulling the same thread across different centuries. Every new thing you learn gets added and immediately inherits every connection already in the web. It stops being notes. It becomes a second brain of political knowledge where any idea can be followed in any direction across any century.
### Party Organizer's Schedule
- You have an Events cell. Inside it you have individual event cells — "Johnson Wedding," "Maya's Sweet 16," "Rooftop Corporate June 14." Inside each event you have Tasks as children, each task its own cell. Each task gets spoused to a "complete" or "incomplete" cell whose parent is Status. Each task also gets spoused to a category cell — "Catering," "Decor," "Vendors," "Venue."
- Query for all tasks across all events spoused to "incomplete" and get every outstanding task across your entire workload at once. Hone it — query for children of "Johnson Wedding" spoused to both "incomplete" and "Catering" and get every unfinished catering task for that one event specifically. Maybe you are writing a brief for your assistant covering everything that needs to happen before the weekend — you toss that query inline into the document and the results populate right there. Every outstanding vendor task across every active event, listed inside the document where you are already writing the brief.
###  Woodworker's Brain Dump
- A client wants a dining table. You make a cell for the commission. Inside it you have Materials, Joinery, Dimensions, Constraints as children. "White oak" is a cell inside Materials. "Breadboard end" is a cell inside Joinery, spoused to "white oak" because that joint choice was made specifically for that wood's expansion behavior. "Drawbore peg" is a child of "breadboard end" — the specific technique used to execute that joint. "72 inches" is a dimension cell spoused to "white oak" and "breadboard end" because that length informed both choices.
- Now you also have a Constraints cell — "client has young children" is a child, spoused to "finish: hardwax oil" inside a Finishes cell because that constraint drove that finish choice, and spoused to "no sharp apron profile" inside Joinery. Every decision in the commission is declared alongside the reason it was made and the other decisions it connects to. Three months later the client wants a matching bench. You query for all cells in the original commission spoused to "white oak" and every decision made because of or in relation to that wood surfaces — joint choices, finish, dimension tolerances. The bench inherits the logic of the table.
