## Why EE?
- I chose electrical engineering because I love software. This might seem counter intuitive, but, it makes perfect sense. I love software for its ability to bring abstract concepts to fruition. Its a context free from the shackles and constraints of reality and physics. Its the perfect medium to engage with these concepts and exact immense power and potential. This is all well and good but software is imprisoned in the realm of the digital. To bridge the gap between digital and real, we need hardware. That is what I am interested in studying and contributing to: summoning forth the nigh-infinite power of the digital dimension to the physical plane.

- This sounds more like computer engineering which you could make the case for. An education in electrical engineering gives me a much better understanding of the hardware concepts which are much harder to learn than their software counterparts. 
- Additionally, I've really come to love physics. Its the perfect example of an emergent system. Abstraction as a means to an end. An abstract system modeling tangible components (unlike math which is abstraction as a means for more abstraction). 

## Future Goals
### Applied Physics Doctorate
- After my bachelors, I've been heavily considering pursuing grad school, most likely a PhD. I've become very disillusioned over the past year, now being at college, with the industry. What's really done this is the constant slew of career fairs and events where I just hear the same nonsense about networking and linkedin. It all just feels so disengenuine. Many of the people I meet in engineering are just kind of doing it because they like it and it paid the best. I recently did a hackathon that solidified my uneasy feelings about working as an engineer. I realized I really like just building things as a hobby. Engineering as a hobby. It's akin to painting. You may love to do it without any bounds or constraints. But the fun is taken away when you're given a specific prompt and forced to work with others who don't care about the painting or have completely different art styles. I joined engineering not for the money but because I love to build new things and that's my passion. The main issue I find is that so much of the industry seems nongenuine and like people are just making stuff to make stuff, I want to be in a field where everyone else loves whwat they do with all their heart and works toward something larger than just meeting the next weeks profit qouta. And in most of the places I'd get work, I wouldn't be able to do that. Smaller startups and research labs being the exception. Beyond this too, I believe the value of working in some field career wise is you get to do the things you love on a larger scale. With engineering I felt like it was the opposite I predict I'll be almost limited in aspects.
- This brings me to my next point regarding research. When I was very young, I was obssesed with these inventors and just loved the idea of being able to build new things. I would always say I wanted to be an inventor. So when the time came to kind of solidify a career path, I gravitated towards engineering because I thought that would be the closest. But this first year of school has showed me, you won't likely be inventing anything new or even neccesarily creating something that altogether innovative. In most engineering jobs, I'd just be implenting existing things. And even if I am innovating, its still very close to the norm. What I was looking for was being able to create entirely new things and work on burgeoning fields. This is why I've become very interested in working in research labs like the IBM Quantam Computing lab and NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (to name a few well known examples). These places would allow me to do things I would never be able to do on my own (as opposed to writing code or building electronics). I'd be in once-in-a-lifetime envirnoments filled with brilliant people working on groundbreaking work. But to do all this, in a fashion not neccesarily tied to electrical engineering (circuits and software), I need some level of higher education.
- For this reason, I've chosen to do a doctorate in Applied Physics. Applied physics is kind of exactly where I want to be, its the middleground between discovery and application, between science and engineering. It will open up a lot of doors and sounds incredibly exciting. Also, because of other past experiences this year and being closer to research, I've began to love the vibe of academia and research. Everyone really cares and is working on such niche, interesting things. Also, suprisingly, the pace is much more chill than industry.
  - As a note, I have had minimal experience with Physics (only taking first year courses) but baesd on a lot of indicators I have a very very strong prediction that I will like it and that's what I've been gravitating towards (from a career standpoint). I'll be testing that theory this summer. 
  - Additionally, the oppurtunities and life trajectory I could get through that would be leagues more fulfilling and unique compared to just an engineering degree. 
- So what does this mean for my engineering degree? Well, I think I'm mostly doing it for fun. I just genuinely enjoy studying the material and think a degree in engineering is useful in case other things don't work out. And, learning more about all this stuff and having university resources allows me to build bigger and better hardware/software personal creations.
