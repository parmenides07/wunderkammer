*In a world once like yours...*
                 
Braise was fast—but not quite as fast as the Hookweaver ahead of him. It had eluded all his attempts at capture. Every time his spear was sure to pierce its scaly exterior, the fish would already be yards ahead. It's tendrils would lash out, latching onto the waves in front. First, it'd match the sea's drift then, shortly after, it'd thrust its body up and out of the water. It'd cut through the air at incredibly speeds then eventually complete its arc and fall back in. With just one swing of its pearlescent extremities, the creature would be propelled nearly out of sight. 

For the ones he'd already killed, rather than blindly pursue, Braise had chose a more guileful strategy—one that capitalized on all of the Hookweaver's weaknesses: it's memory spanning only to what it last ate; it relying on the surrounding water's displacement for sight; and it having to attach to other Hookweavers for proper respiration. These biological shortcomings allowed Braise to make quick work of the first three in the pack. For those, he had handled them in the midst of a wollastinitic field, a territory interspersed with rings of wollastanite, a bacteria that settles on the sea surface at the wave boundaries, feeding on the sea foam that emerges. Their chemical composition forces the liquid they inhabit to take on a gelatinous property. A property that dampens the displacement of the water, effectively blinding any Hookweavers caught amidst it. They'd stumble through the ring and eventually find themselves in the center—exactly where Braise had positioned his ship. Carefully balanced on the ship's mast, he'd raise a spiraled spearstaff in his one hand, leaving the other empty to shield his eyes from the glare atop the glass waves. As soon as the water became colored with the presence of the Hookweaver packmass, he'd leap off the mast and land upon it, piercing the unit with the spiraled spear. He'd give thanks to Messiah as he pushed his weapon through the fishpack. 

This procedure worked flawlessly for three among the pack, ripping through their entrails and clouding the water with various green bodily fluids. However, the last of the pack was much more stubborn in accepting its defeat. The moment before the spear marked its target, the fish had forsook its brethren, leaping off to escape its predator. And it almost did if it hadn't been for the loss of speed from now moving alone. Alone it had only a few tendrils pulling it's body through the water. That kept Braise in close pursuit. 

Despite struggling to keep up, every so often he was able to lurch forward and throw the spear like a javelin, it danced and spun through the water, slicing through the liquid and gaining momentum. Every time, though, the Hookweaver would dodge just before it was hit. He believed that surely The Furies must be cursing him. He'd return their curses with his own, cursing "damn those three!" after every miss. With a gesture, he's return the spear to his hand, praying to the Messiah and continuing his fruitless pursuit.
